**In preparation** — targeting ICLR 2027. (First author; * corresponding author.)

A multimodal LLM can answer a visual question correctly either by reading the image or by guessing from priors, and correctness alone does not separate the two. The distinction is testable in principle — edit the region carrying the evidence and see whether the answer changes — but that test needs an edited image, which no deployed system has at inference time.

**GLANCE** estimates the outcome of that counterfactual from the untouched image. Given a candidate evidence region *R*, it scores the same answer three times — under a crop of *R*, under the full image, and under a view with *R* masked out — adding two forward passes to a frozen model with no training and no labels.

On a human-annotated anchor it separates grounded from guessed-correct answers at **AUROC 0.812 / 0.798**, exceeding the strongest of nine region-blind baselines by **+0.263 / +0.251**. The separation belongs to the input family rather than to any one formula: every prior-cancelling contrast that can see *R* lands within 0.03–0.05 of the headline, while every region-blind signal stays near chance. Rebuilding every label with a different model family reproduces the result, and removing the candidate answer set entirely leaves it significant on freely generated answers (0.758 / 0.723).

The score measures **evidence use** and supports abstention on grounding risk; it does not rank correctness.
