**In preparation** — targeting ICLR 2027. (* corresponding author.)

RedDust is a 30-day survival-shelter benchmark that scores LLM agents not only on whether they survive, but on whether they stay accountable under pressure. Its three axes — survival/resources, auditability, and narrative ethics — are kept as an un-merged, floor-gated profile so that a strong axis cannot mask a failure elsewhere.

Its headline result is a **decorrelation**: short-horizon social skill does not predict long-horizon consistency. On a frozen 30-day scenario, a 13-agent panel spanning six real model families (DeepSeek, Claude, Gemini, GLM, Kimi, MiniMax) plus four deterministic references gives Pearson 0.81 / Spearman 0.65 with 18 rank reversals — and at *matched* short-horizon social skill, families split between sustaining long-horizon promise-keeping and collapsing on it. A three-arm permutation control (endogenous 0.81 vs. shuffle-null 0 ± 0.29, two-tailed p = 0.001) separates the real association from a pairing artifact.

The cross-model figure is single-seed and the integrity axis is report-only pending a natural-hard-case κ; both limitations, along with the engine and evaluation design, are detailed on the project page: [RedDust — LLM-Agent Accountability &amp; Trust Benchmark](/portfolio/2-reddust/).
